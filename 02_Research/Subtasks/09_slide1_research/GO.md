### Eli's design requirements (added Nov 14; these are binding)
Eli reads this workbook first thing in the morning to form his own views. It must be **organized, intuitive, clearly labeled and polished**, not a data dump.

**1. Two layers:**
- **Raw tabs** (prefix `raw_`, grouped at the end, grey tab color): every raw series behind a slide 1 number, visible in the workbook. Each one has its source file path, URL, vintage and retrieval date in the header rows. Minimum set:
  - `raw_PCE_T20804` (index levels, core and headline, 2017 onward);
  - `raw_PCE_components` (the Sep 2026 U20404/U20405 component levels and weights used for breadth and the gap);
  - `raw_Oct_build` (the October estimate's component table);
  - `raw_CPI` (the CPI items used in R3);
  - `raw_BEA_9_1U`;
  - `raw_backtest`;
  - `raw_outside_estimates`;
  - `raw_Cleveland`;
  - `raw_BEA_release_text` (the R4 sentences, quoted).
- **Research tabs** (one per item, colored tabs): these **draw from the raw tabs with live Excel formulas** wherever that's practical, e.g. 1m/3m/6m/12m rates, breadth shares, median and range, bp distance, gap sums. That way Eli can click any number and trace it to raw data.
- Where a calculation is too complex for formulas (for example the Fisher decomposition), write the value from the script, and put a cell note naming the script and function.

**2. Native Excel charts** (openpyxl charts, built from the workbook's own cells; never images) wherever a picture carries economic meaning. Each chart gets a title, axis titles with units, a source line, and a 2% target line where relevant. Minimum:
- `TS_PCE_rates`: core and headline 12m line, 2018 onward, with the 2% line, plus a core 3m vs 6m vs 12m line chart for the last 24 months. October is marked est.
- `Headline`: a bar chart of the core rates at 1m-annualized, 3m, 6m and 12m, with backtest bands as error bars if feasible.
- `R1_backtest`: our error vs Cleveland's vs CPI-alone's, by month.
- `R2_outside_estimates`: a dot or bar chart of core m/m by forecaster, with ours highlighted and the median line.
- `R3_core_gap`: a waterfall-style stacked bar from core CPI to core PCE, by effect.
- `R6_breadth`: the breadth time series (3m and 12m), with the 2018–19 average line, plus a Sep vs Oct bar by method.
- Add others where they help Eli see the data. Skip any chart that adds nothing.

**3. Readability on every research tab:**
- A1: a plain-language title.
- A2: a one-line "what this tab shows", data only, no interpretation.
- A3: units, vintage, and which raw tab it draws from.
- Consistent number formats (2 decimals for rates, 1 for breadth), frozen header rows, readable column widths, bold headers, light banding.
- `est.` and `carried forward` flagged with a consistent fill color, explained in the README legend.
- The `README` tab is a clickable table of contents (hyperlinks to each tab) with a one-line description of each.

**4. Verification:** after building, recalculate the formulas if LibreOffice is available:
```
soffice --headless --convert-to xlsx --outdir <scratchpad>
```
Then check that every formula cell matches the script's value to 1e-6. If LibreOffice isn't available, have the script compute the same values and write a hidden `check` tab comparing them, and say so in FINAL.md. Zero formula errors (#REF!, #DIV/0!) allowed.

**Tab order:** research tabs first, raw tabs last:

1. `README`: purpose, vintages, build date, how to rebuild, the label key ("est." = our estimate; "carried forward"; "press-reported").
2. `Headline`: Oct 2026 estimate, core and headline. Rates at 1m, 3m ann., 6m ann. and 12m, with backtest bands. Sep→Oct m/m.
3. `TS_PCE_rates`: monthly core and headline PCE at 1m, 3m ann., 6m ann. and 12m. Jan 2018 through Sep 2026 published (BEA, Nov 10 vintage), plus Oct 2026 flagged `est.`. Long and wide layouts, one row per month in the wide one.
4. `R1_backtest`: month-by-month backtest (ours, Cleveland, core CPI alone vs BEA first print), plus summary MAE and bias.
5. `R2_outside_estimates`: every outside estimate with source, timestamp, tier (primary / press-reported / model) and pre- or post-PPI. Median, range, and our distance in bp, computed in cells or by the script.
6. `R3_core_gap`: core CPI–PCE gap decomposition, Sep and Oct.
7. `R4_sep_pce_check`: Sep headline and core y/y from index levels (3 decimals) vs BEA's release text vs each official's stated figure.
8. `R5_cleveland`: Cleveland nowcast, labeled **"carried forward: Nov 14 pull; re-pull Nov 18"**.
9. `R6_breadth`: Sep and Oct breadth, 3m and 12m, core and headline; the sub-line primary and leaf robustness; the component-sum gap split.
10. `Sources`: every raw file and URL behind each tab.
11. `Judgment_calls_and_paywalled`: judgment calls and paywalled links.

Formatting: data blocks start below the three header rows. No merged cells inside data blocks. Numbers are stored as numbers. After the research tabs come the `raw_` tabs listed above.

The Codex audit (step 4) also checks that each research tab's formulas point at the right raw cells, and that each chart plots the series its title claims.
