## Slide 1: Where is inflation, and is it accelerating?

**Question:** How far above target is inflation, and is the recent direction up, flat or down?

**Examines:**
- Headline and core PCE at 12-month, 6-month annualized and 3-month annualized rates, through September (the last official PCE before Nov 20).
- **October PCE**, with two nowcasts side by side, both labeled as estimates: our own build and the Cleveland Fed's. This follows Harvard's two-model approach. The gap between them shows model disagreement; it isn't a confidence interval.
- October CPI (core and headline, short horizons) as confirmation. Label the 12-month figure as approximate, because the October 2025 base was interpolated.
- Optional passing line: "On Oct 5 we set a trigger: reacceleration in core. Here's what the data show."

**Spoken job:** set the problem in the first 60 seconds, because inflation opens the presentation.

**Research needed:** Sep PCE horizons (in the pipeline); the Oct PCE build (in flight); the Cleveland nowcast as of the cutoff; the Oct CPI view (built).

## Slide 2: What's driving it, and is it spreading beyond energy?

**Question:** Is this a one-time energy level shift that policy can look through, or is it broadening into the rest of the basket?

**Examines:**
- **Contributions** to headline and core by group: energy, food, core goods, housing, core services ex housing. Is the rise one sector or many?
- **Breadth:** share of the core basket above 3%, at 12 months vs. 3 months, shown with contributions so one heavy item can't drive the reading unseen.
- **The energy pipeline in the PPI:** crude/unprocessed → processed intermediate → final demand → consumer prices. Is cost pressure still building upstream? October PPI was released today.
- **Pass-through:** the FEDS note's post-pandemic estimate (panel of five economies, upper bound), plus the US observable that would confirm it: energy-intensive core items such as airfares, transportation and food away from home.
- Optional: a supply-vs-demand split of core inflation (the SF Fed decomposition was on the Oct 5 deck). It speaks directly to whether policy can act on the drivers.

**Spoken job:** the energy story, plus the honest question of whether it is leaking into core.

**Research needed:** contributions and breadth, rebuilt **after** the core-flag fix (`05_fix_core_flag`); Oct PPI; the second half of the FEDS note (unverified).

## Slide 3: Will it persist? Underlying inflation, wages and expectations

**Question:** Is inflation becoming self-sustaining, through sticky prices, wages or expectations?

**Examines:**
- **Underlying measures:** supercore PCE; market-based core services ex housing; Atlanta sticky vs. flexible CPI; trimmed mean and median. The trimmed mean is the strongest contrary reading, so show it here rather than leave it for Q&A.
- **Wages and unit labor costs:** ECI (Employment Cost Index), unit labor costs. Are labor costs feeding prices?
- **Expectations:**
  - short vs. long horizons;
  - households (Michigan, NY Fed survey) vs. professionals (Q4 SPF) vs. markets (breakevens, 5y5y).
  - Pace's framing is useful here: expectations feed wage and price setting through the Phillips curve.
- Optional if space allows: shelter, new-lease vs. measured rents (a leading indicator of the slowest-moving component).

**Spoken job:** the hinge between hike and hold. A look-through case needs anchored expectations and contained wages.

**Research needed:** pipeline refreshes plus the Q4 SPF (mid-Nov); the question log's open item on trimmed mean vs. breadth.

## Slide 4: AI demand and the inflation outlook

**Question:** Is the AI build-out adding demand-driven cost pressure that monetary policy can act on? And given everything in slides 1–4, where is inflation going?

**Examines:**
- **AI-related input prices in the PPI.** Examples: semiconductors and electronic components, electrical equipment (transformers, switchgear), data-center construction inputs, and electric power generation and transmission. Are they accelerating faster than other inputs?
- **Does it reach consumer prices?** Most of these inputs feed investment goods, not the PCE basket. The main consumer-facing channel to test is electricity.
- **Is it rate-sensitive?** Indicators of how AI investment is funded and how it responded to the September hike, for example capex growth and data-center construction since the hike.
- **The offset:** productivity and unit labor costs (Pace's productivity point). Treat this as consistent with an AI effect, not evidence of one: AI can't be attributed in the official data.
- **Forecast:** a baseline core PCE path through 2027 from labeled sources (September SEP, Q4 SPF, the October nowcast), with upside and downside scenarios. Upside: oil pass-through persists, AI demand keeps growing, expectations drift. Downside: oil reverses, equity-linked services drag.
- **Close:** a one-sentence balance-of-risks judgment, Pace-style, that carries into the labor and growth sections.

**Spoken job:** end the section with where inflation is heading, and leave the "can we afford to act?" question for the next sections.

**Research needed:** a PPI series list for AI inputs (new; to be scoped); data on capex funding and rate sensitivity; the forecast sources.
