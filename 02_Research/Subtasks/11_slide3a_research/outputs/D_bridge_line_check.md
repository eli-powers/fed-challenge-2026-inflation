## Clause 1: "Upstream pressure doesn't show second-round effects"
**Verdict: supported, with one logical caveat.**

For:
- The oil note: "second-round effects have not yet materialized; however, risks are mounting" (`C_q10_oil_note_verification.csv` row 17) [slide 2 C].
- Labor costs quiet: ULC 8q 1.92 vs 2018–19 1.90; ECI W&S y/y 3.12 [B]; V/U 1.06 < BB's 1.2 [A].
- Long-run market expectations flat: 5y5y 2.15 (Feb 2026) → 2.19 (Nov) (`D_q9_checks.csv` rows 28–30) [O].

Against / caveats:
- Upstream PPI cannot distinguish first- from second-round mechanisms on its own: PPI prices can embed wages and expectations, but the stage data do not identify which (wording corrected per Codex R1-F05). A judge may hear the clause as "we looked and found none" when the measure could not have found any. The evidence of absence comes from wages, ULC and expectations (3A), not from upstream.
- One upstream sub-index points at services costs: inputs to stage-1 goods producers, services (WPSID5112), 3m ann. 35.5, y/y 19.2, both the highest since 2018 (`C_q8_ppi_stages_summary.csv` row 86) [slide 2 C]. Services inputs embed wages; not decomposed.
- "Record" in slide 2's upstream framing is the stage 1 **index level**; stage 1 y/y 14.1 ranks 16th of 106 months (row 12).

## Clause 2: "but it lengthens the first round"
**Verdict: supported by lag evidence and the oil note's dynamics; weakened by the November oil reversal.**

For:
- Stage 1 Oct: +0.55 m/m, 18.9 3m ann., 14.1 y/y (`C_q8_ppi_stages_summary.csv` row 12) [slide 2 C].
- Lagged pass-through: truck freight +3.9% in Oct (NSA) while diesel fell; truck-to-food correlations peak at 7–8-month lags (slide 2 FINAL Q11; descriptive, not causal) [slide 2 C].
- Oil note: most of the core price-level effect builds within ~8 quarters; post-pandemic pass-through 0.014 vs 0.001 (`C_q10_lit_summary_update.md`) [slide 2 C].
- Slide 2: energy-exposed items' mean 12m rate 3.14 → 3.92 → 4.30 (Feb → Sep → Oct est.) (`D_preshock.csv`) [slide 2 D].

Against:
- Brent fell to $91.84 (Nov 4) and $92.14 (Nov 11), back near the August level (`C_q8_oil_daily.csv`) [slide 2 C]. Oct stage 2 −0.21 m/m; unprocessed goods (ID62) −3.08; energy's contribution to stage 1 turned negative (−0.25 pp) [slide 2 FINAL].
- The data fit "the first round got bigger" as well as "longer" (slide 2 FINAL §0.1). Length is inferred from lags, not observed.
- Short-run expectations already fell in Nov prelim (Michigan 1y 4.7 → 4.3) as gas fell [C].

## Clause 3: "which raises their risk"
**Verdict: supported conceptually and by BB's model; the empirical estimates of how much are weak.**

For:

- BB printed p.18: short-run expectations "feed fairly quickly into wages" [O]. SCE 1y +0.91 pp and Michigan 1y +0.9 pp since Feb 2026; SCE 3y +0.29 (`D_q9_checks.csv` rows 16, 24, 27) [O].
- Duration: 67 months above 2% through Sep (`C_q8_time_above_summary.csv` row 2) [C].
- Catch-up motive: real ECI W&S −0.53 pp vs headline PCE (`D_q9_checks.csv` row 85) [O]; Beige Book cost-of-living wage demands [B]; NFIB compensation plans 24, highest since Dec 2025 [B].
- Oil note: "risks are mounting" (row 17) [slide 2 C].

Against:
- BB: catch-up ≈ 0 (Table 2, p=0.765); short-run expectations react slowly (0.124, Table 4); no de-anchoring in 2021–23 even after a much larger shock (Table 5); price-shock effects "quite short-lived" (p.30) [A].
- V/U 1.06 below 1.2: BB's main amplifier is weak [A].
- The oil note cannot separate a changed inflation process from more frequent/persistent shocks (row 25); its higher persistence might not apply forward.
- No hard-data series shows wage catch-up yet (ECI, AHE y/y, ULC) [B].

## Net
Clauses 1 and 3 have direct textual support (oil note; BB p.9 and p.18). Clause 2 rests on lag evidence that the November oil drop partly undercuts. The weakest logical link is clause 1: upstream data cannot distinguish first- from second-round mechanisms on their own, so the "doesn't show" needs the 3A labor and expectations evidence behind it.
