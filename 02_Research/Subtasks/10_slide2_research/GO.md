## Research questions (what the briefs must cover)

**A. Tags and attribution**
- **Q1. Finish the tags.** Verify the 2025 reciprocal-tariff exemption for computers and smartphones from a primary source (proclamation, Federal Register or CBP guidance). Spot-check the energy tags against the BEA input-output tables. Report any tag that should change. Don't change `tag_final` itself: propose changes for Eli.
- **Q2. Bowman-style attribution.** Split above-target core PCE (core minus 2%) into the contributions of the tagged groups (energy, tariff, AI) and untagged items, for Sep (published) and Oct (est.), at 12m and 3m annualized.
  - Compare with Bowman's "roughly a quarter each": quote her exact words from the saved speech.
  - State the method, including how the 2% is allocated across groups; test at least one alternative allocation.
- **Q3. Tagged vs untagged breadth.** Weighted-leaf breadth inside each tag group and for untagged items, 3m and 12m, Sep vs Oct, plus a monthly time series from 2018. Run the strict 5% energy cutoff and the "all transport" sensitivity. Decompose the Oct 12m breadth drop by tag group: the hospital lines are untagged.

**B. Breadth measures (R9, Eli's emphasis)**
- **Q4. Breadth by measure.**
  - Weighted-leaf breadth for **core** (from slide 1) and **supercore** (core services excluding housing), Sep vs Oct, 3m and 12m. Monthly from 2018, with 2018–19 averages.
  - Add core goods and core services. Headline goes to backup only, because it rises mechanically in an energy shock.
- **Q4b. Breadth vs trimmed mean** (questions log, Nov 12: "a judge will ask which reading is right").
  - About 60% of core weight has 12m inflation above 3% (Sep), yet the Dallas 12m trimmed mean is about 2.3%. Its upper-trim count rose from 70 to 85 components.
  - Reconcile the two: their constructions differ (12m component rates vs monthly trimming).
  - Answer in two or three sentences of data, ready for Q&A.
  - Optional measure: Eli's "harmonized PCE" (core excluding OER) breadth, if it adds anything. That series is rerun each release; see the memory note.
- **Q5. Sticky vs flexible (exploratory; Eli: fine if it shows nothing).**
  - Map PCE lines to the Atlanta Fed sticky/flexible CPI classification through the saved BLS PCE–CPI concordance (`03_Data/Raw/BLS_pce_cpi_code_mapping_2026-11-11.csv`), using approach (a).
  - Lines priced from PPI or other non-CPI sources are **left out**. Report the covered share of core weight.
  - Compute sticky and flexible breadth, Sep vs Oct.
  - Cross-check with breadth on the Atlanta Fed's own sticky-price CPI basket, which needs no mapping. Download the classification from the Atlanta Fed.
  - If coverage is poor or the result is noisy, say so plainly.
  - Context (log, Nov 10): the Atlanta Fed flexible CPI was 10.2 (Aug), 22.2 (Sep) and 3.0 (Oct), 1m annualized. Include its sticky and flexible series as a reference panel.
- **Q6. Waller comparison.** On core services 12m, give every variant: count and weighted, leaf and sub-line, Sep and Oct, pulled from slide 1 (`C_waller_variants.csv`). Add Oct if it isn't there. The weighted leaf share is Eli's preferred basis, because it reproduces Waller's ">70%".
- **Q7. The hospital base effect.**
  - Did BEA fill the Oct 2025 hospital value? October 2025 was the shutdown month. Check the PPI series `PCU622---622---` for Oct 2025 (published, or missing and imputed) and BEA's note on how it handled missing October 2025 source data.
  - Answer: does the +0.89% Oct 2025 jump reflect a real price move or a fill?
  - This decides whether the verbal "base effect" line can be said.
  - Revision risk (log, Nov 12): the hospital PPI is used without a first release. Aug 2026 was +0.62% m/m at first print and +0.22% now. Report how revision-prone this series is, and whether the Oct 2026 hospital estimate (0.305%) could plausibly flip the 12m breadth result (it needs 0.564% to stay above 3%).
