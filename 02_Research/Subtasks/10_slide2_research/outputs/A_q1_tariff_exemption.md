# Q1.1: Did the 2025 reciprocal tariffs exempt computers and smartphones? Did the exemption still stand?

Tags: [O] = opened and text extracted locally (saved in `03_Data/Raw/tariff_exemption_2026-11-15/`), [S] = search snippet only, [2] = secondary source. Dates are as published.

## Short answer
- **Yes.** A presidential memorandum of **April 11, 2025** put HTS 8471 (computers) and 8517.13.00 (smartphones), 8542 (integrated circuits) and other headings inside the "semiconductors" exception already written into the reciprocal-tariff order (E.O. 14257), effective retroactively to **April 5, 2025**. [O]
- **The reciprocal tariffs themselves no longer exist.** The Supreme Court held on **Feb 20, 2026** that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, which ended the E.O. 14257 tariffs. [O syllabus] The replacements carried the same electronics exemption:
  - Section 122 surcharge (10%, Feb 24 to Jul 24, 2026, Proclamation 11012): the exemption list (note 2(aa)(ii), heading 9903.03.03) includes 8471.xx and 8517.13.00. [O]
  - Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs (10% or 12.5%, from Jul 24, 2026; FR notice 2026-15181, published Jul 28): the exemption list (heading 9903.05.86) includes 8471.xx and 8517.13.00. [O]
- **Section 232 semiconductors did not replace the exemption for finished computers or phones.** Proclamation 11002 (Jan 14, 2026, effective Jan 15) imposes 25% only on "a very narrow category of semiconductors" (advanced AI chips) and does not apply to non-data-center consumer applications. A broader "second phase" was recommended but I found no confirmation it had been imposed by Nov 15, 2026 (unverified, [S]).
- **Latest date confirmed:** the Section 301 notice in force from Jul 24, 2026 (text read [O]). Nothing after that date was verified. I did not check whether USTR or the President changed the lists after Jul 28.

## 1. The April 2025 exemption [O]
- **Document:** "Memorandum on Clarification of Exceptions Under Executive Order 14257 of April 2, 2025, as Amended," April 11, 2025 (Compilation of Presidential Documents, DCPD202500470). Local file: `03_Data/Raw/tariff_exemption_2026-11-15/DCPD-202500470.pdf`. URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202500470/pdf/DCPD-202500470.pdf
- It is a **memorandum**, not an executive order (many outlets call it an order). The govinfo copy carries the note "An original was not available for verification of the content of this memorandum." I did not locate it in the Federal Register.
- **Exact language:**
  - "One of those excepted products is 'semiconductors.'"
  - "That term's meaning includes the products classified in the following headings and subheadings of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS):"
  - HTS list: 8471; 847330; 8486; 85171300; 85176200; 85235100; 8524; 85285200; 85411000; 85412100; 85412900; 85413000; 85414910; 85414970; 85414980; 85414995; 85415100; 85415900; 85419000; 8542.
  - Effective date: "effective as of 12:01 a.m. eastern daylight time on April 5, 2025", with refunds of duties collected on those goods since that time.
- The memo does not use the words "computers" or "smartphones". The HTS descriptions do: 8471 = automatic data processing machines (computers, tablets, peripherals); 85171300 = "Smartphones for cellular networks or for other wireless networks" (description from the Federal Register annex of 2026-03824, [O], p.18 of the PDF).
- CBP implemented it through CSMS message 64724565 [S, search snippet only; not opened].
- Fit with our tags: PCE leaf 49 (PCs/tablets/peripherals) and leaf 71 (telephones) map to these headings. Leaf 49's direct import share is 57% and leaf 71's is 37% (`08_shock_tagging/proposed_tags.csv`), but these goods carried no reciprocal-tariff duty from Apr 5, 2025. The AI+tariff "overlap" flag on those leaves therefore reflects import share, not an actual tariff. The AI-ahead-of-tariff ordering does not conflict with the exemption. Caveat: other tariffs not checked here (the China Section 301 tariffs from 2018-19, and Section 232 steel/aluminum derivatives) could still touch some of these goods.

## 2. End of the reciprocal tariffs and the carry-over of the exemption
- **SCOTUS, Learning Resources v. Trump, No. 24-1287, decided Feb 20, 2026** [O: syllabus extracted from the slip opinion, `SCOTUS_learning_resources_v_trump.pdf`]. Holding: "IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs." Vote 6-3 [2: Ropes & Gray, DLA Piper and others via search]. URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf
- **Proclamation 11012** (Feb 20, 2026; 91 FR 9339, published Feb 25), "Imposing a Temporary Import Surcharge To Address Fundamental International Payments Problems" [O: DCPD202600129 and the FR public-inspection PDF]. A 10% surcharge "for a period of 150 days ... effective February 24, 2026" (ends July 24, 2026). Paragraph 14(g) lists "certain electronics" as exempt, and 14(k) exempts "all articles and parts of articles currently or that later become subject to ... section 232". Annex I, U.S. note 2(aa)(ii) (heading 9903.03.03) says the surcharge "shall not apply to articles the product of any country that are classifiable in the following subheadings", and the list contains 8471.30.01, 8471.41.01, 8471.49.00, 8471.50.01, 8471.60.10 through 8471.90.00, 8473.30.xx, 8486.xx, 8517.13.00, 8517.62.00, 8523.51.00, 8524.xx, 8541.xx, 8542.31 to 8542.90. Local copy: `FR_2026-03824_public_inspection.pdf` (pages 15-18). URL: https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-03824.pdf
- **Section 301 forced-labor tariffs**, USTR notice 2026-15181 (FR publication Jul 28, 2026; duties effective 12:01 a.m. ET Jul 24, 2026 [O; the notice text says "on or after 12:01 a.m. eastern time on July 24, 2026"]). The notice, in Annex I, says the duties of headings 9903.05.20 to 9903.05.84 "shall not apply to articles that are classifiable in the following provisions of the HTSUS" (heading 9903.05.86). I confirmed that the list includes 8471.30.01 to 8471.90.00, 8473.30.xx, 8486.xx, 8517.13.00, 8517.62.00, 8523.51.00, 8524.xx, 8541.xx, 8542.31.00 to 8542.90.00 (and sits before the next paragraph "(c)", p.84 of the PDF). Local copy: `FR_2026-15181_public_inspection.pdf` (383 pages). URL: https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-15181.pdf. Rates: 10%, 12.5%, or net of MFN for some economies; the USTR press release of Jul 23, 2026 gives country rates [O]: https://www.ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations
- **Section 232 semiconductors**, Proclamation 11002 (Jan 14, 2026; effective Jan 15) [O: DCPD202600026]. Quotes: the Secretary recommended "a 25 percent ad valorem tariff on a very narrow category of semiconductors"; the duty rate does not apply to imports "for non-data center consumer applications in the United States". The "second phase ... broader tariffs on semiconductors" was recommended "after trade negotiations have concluded". Commerce was to report by July 1, 2026. Whether phase two was imposed by Nov 15: unverified (searched; a [S] result says it was still pending in mid-2026; nothing found for Oct-Nov 2026).

## 3. What this means for the tags (data only)
- The tariff tag is defined by direct import share (>=15%) and goods share, not by actual tariff incidence. Under that rule, PCs and phones (import shares 57% and 37%) would be tariff-tagged; they are AI-tagged first by priority. They were exempt from reciprocal/Section 122/Section 301-forced-labor duties throughout, so they carry no tariff double-count.
- The legal basis for the tariff group changed during the window we measure: IEEPA reciprocal rates (Apr 2025 to Feb 2026), then Section 122 (Feb 24 to Jul 24, 2026), then Section 301 (from Jul 24). Section 232 duties (autos, steel and aluminum derivatives, etc.) ran throughout. Passenger vehicles and 232 goods are exempt from the Section 122 and Section 301-forced-labor layers because they have 232 duties instead. [O: Proclamation 11012 para. 14(h),(k); FR 2026-15181 preamble]
